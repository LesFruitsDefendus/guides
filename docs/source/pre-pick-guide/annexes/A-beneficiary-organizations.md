# Annex A: Beneficiary organizations

A beneficiary organization is a partner to whom we donate 1/3 of the fruit we harvest during a pick. Beneficiary organizations ensure the redistribution of the fruit to those in need and promote food security.

During the harvest season, our list of beneficiary organizations will be updated regularly, therefore, you should refer to the most current version on Saskatoon.

## Decision Tree: Where Should You Donate Your Harvest?

1. **Identify potential recipient organizations**
    - Open the list of beneficiary organizations in the software.
    - Filter by the neighbourhood where your harvest is taking place to identify our partners located closest to your tree.

2. **Assess the quantity and quality of your harvest**

    There are several factors to consider:
    - **Quantity:** Is it a small harvest, a substantial harvest, or more fruit than one organization can receive?
    - **Quality:** Is the fruit in good condition and ready to eat, or is it overripe, bruised, spotted, or otherwise better suited to processing?
    - **Shelf life:** How quickly does the fruit need to be consumed or processed?

3. **Match the harvest to the right organization**

    **Good-quality fruit that can be eaten as is**

    For substantial quantities of fruit in good condition, prioritize food banks and other organizations that distribute fresh produce directly to their users.

    **Fruit that needs processing before consumption**

    Collective kitchens and organizations that offer food-processing activities are generally better suited to receiving fruit that is overripe, bruised, spotted, or otherwise requires processing before it can be eaten.

    **Very large harvests**

    If your harvest exceeds the receiving capacity of a single organization, consider:
    - Dividing the donation among several organizations;
    - Donating to SADA (de la Corbeille), which redistributes food through its network of community organizations.

    **Very small harvests**

    For small quantities of berries, such as chokeberries, elderberries, goji berries, or sometimes serviceberries, consider:
    - **Community fridges:** Always label the fruit with its type, origin, and harvest date.
    - **Sharing within the harvesting group:** If the quantity is too small to make a meaningful donation, it is perfectly fine to share the harvest among volunteers and the tree owner.

4. **Contact the organization in advance**

    Whenever possible, contact the organization before making the donation. Confirm that it can receive the type, quantity, and quality of fruit you are offering.

    You can also donate to a community organization or initiative that is not yet listed in our software. The same principles outlined above apply: the goal is to ensure that the fruit will be enjoyed, valued, and redistributed for free. If you would like to add a new beneficiary organization to our network, simply email me its name and contact details, along with any relevant information, and go ahead with the donation.

    The only organizations that cannot be part of our beneficiary network are those that would privatize the fruit, generate a profit from it, or assign it a monetary value—for example, markets or companies that would sell the fruit. Our network is built around free sharing and redistribution, not the commercialization of our harvests.   

---

**Remember: The goal is to ensure that the fruit is enjoyed, valued, and redistributed for free.**

Our goal is to collectively build a food system based on **decommodified urban fruit-tree commons:** food that circulates through neighbourhoods according to principles of solidarity, equity, and sharing, rather than market-driven or capitalist logic.

Relationships are at the heart of this model. The network we build must be developed intentionally, recognizing the complementary roles and potential synergies among the organizations and people involved.

We want to help create a society of abundance. To make this possible, everyone involved in harvesting needs opportunities to get to know the people behind the community organizations in their neighbourhood and to build meaningful relationships of mutual aid and thoughtful giving.

A successful donation is not just about finding somewhere to drop off fruit. It is about building lasting relationships that help food circulate freely and meaningfully within our communities.
